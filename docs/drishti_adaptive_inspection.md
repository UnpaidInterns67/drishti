# Drishti Adaptive Secondary Inspection

Drishti is not positioned as another face-verification product. It is an
explainable border decision-support layer that connects document integrity,
multi-frame live biometrics, watchlist screening, and identity continuity into
the next safest officer action.

## What makes the workflow different

- Face comparison uses the median result from several quality-passed frames,
  reports the score range, and rejects unstable samples instead of trusting one
  convenient frame.
- Scores inside the configured uncertainty band cannot receive automatic
  approval. Drishti asks for an independent second capture.
- Capture problems produce human instructions for distance, position,
  lighting, and motion rather than a generic failure.
- A known face with conflicting biographic details is shown as an identity
  continuity conflict linked to earlier documents and crossings.
- Every outcome includes a priority, responsible actor, next action, and a
  signal-by-signal explanation. The authorized officer remains responsible for
  the final statutory action.

## Recommended judge demo

1. Run a clean returning-traveller case and show continuity confirmation plus
   the standard-lane recommendation.
2. Deliberately use poor lighting or motion and show real-time corrective
   guidance and stable-frame progress.
3. Demonstrate a borderline face score and show that Drishti refuses a false
   certainty by requesting a second independent biometric capture.
4. Present a same-face/different-profile case and show the relationship graph,
   conflicting fields, supervisor routing, and recorded audit evidence.

The concise pitch is: **"Most systems answer whether two faces match. Drishti
explains whether an identity remains trustworthy across documents, biometrics,
and border crossings—and tells the officer exactly what to verify next."**
